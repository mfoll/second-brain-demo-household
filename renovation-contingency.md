# Renovation Contingency

Money set aside in a renovation budget for what nobody can see or predict: rot, old wiring, supplier price rises. It is not a slush fund for upgrades.

## What We Did

Our $80,000 plan had no line called contingency. The $2,600 "other" line was meant to cover the temporary kitchen, paint and surprises together. The surprises alone were $6,750 ([[renovation-budget-tracker]]): $3,850 for rot, $2,900 for the panel.

## What I Would Use Next Time

- 10% of the contract for a newer house with known systems.
- 15% for a house over 25 years old, or any job that opens floors or touches the electrical service. On our contract that would have been $10,700, comfortably covering what happened.
- Separate line from upgrades. When a contingency pays for nicer tile, it is gone before the rot shows up.
- Pre-check the obvious: the 2011 inspection had flagged the soft floor ([[house-purchase-inspection-2011]]); a load calculation before signing would have priced the panel up front ([[kitchen-electrical-load-calculation]]).

## Where It Applies

The [[roof-replacement]] (decking repairs are the classic hidden cost) and any heat pump work ([[home-energy-retrofits]]).
