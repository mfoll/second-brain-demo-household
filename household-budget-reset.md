# Household Budget Reset

Replace the loose envelope-style sheet we had used since 2019 with a zero-based monthly budget that both of us maintain, and a short monthly meeting to run it.

## Outcome

Reached 2025-04-30: the [[household-budget-spreadsheet]] in use for three full months (February to April), sinking funds funded, and the first-Sunday [[monthly-money-meeting]] held three times without skipping.

## Current State

- Completed. The spreadsheet and the meeting are now routine under [[manage-family-finances]].
- The old sheet is archived as [[budget-spreadsheet-2024-envelopes]].

## Scope and Boundaries

The method and the tool, not the numbers of any later month. Renovation money was out of scope; it got its own tracker later ([[renovation-budget-tracker]]).

## Working Context

Why we did it: in 2024 we ended three months with less cash than we started and could not say where it went. The envelope sheet tracked a few categories and ignored annual bills, so every insurance renewal and car repair felt like an emergency. The renovation was already being discussed, and we wanted to know what we could actually save toward it.

What we chose: a [[zero-based-budgeting]] layout adapted for a household (every dollar assigned each month, not every expense justified from scratch), plus a [[sinking-fund]] line for each irregular cost. Options rejected: a budgeting app with bank sync (we did not want account credentials in another service), and staying with envelopes but adding annual bills (it was the categories that were wrong, not just the missing lines).

What we learned in three months: groceries were $300 a month over what we thought; car costs averaged $210 a month across the year; we could put $1,500 a month toward the renovation fund without touching the emergency fund.
