# Heat Pump Payback

My rough model for whether replacing a gas appliance with a heat pump pays: extra cost after any credit, divided by the yearly saving.

- Water heater (real numbers): extra cost over gas about $700 before credit; after the $735 credit it was slightly cheaper than gas. Saving about $310 a year ([[water-heater-energy-use-comparison]]). Paid back at once, in effect.
- Furnace (guesses, no quotes): a hybrid heat pump might cost $6,000 to $9,000 more than a like-for-like gas furnace. Credit 30% up to $2,000 a year. Saving maybe $400 to $700 a year depending on winters and rates. Payback 7 to 15 years: long, and too uncertain to decide on.

Rules I keep:

- Count the credit only in the year it can actually be claimed; insulation in one tax year and the heat pump in another gets two capped credits.
- Attic insulation before sizing the heat pump ([[building-insulation]]).
- Payback is only one input; replacing before failure has its own value. See [[should-we-replace-the-furnace-with-a-heat-pump]].
