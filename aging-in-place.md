# Aging In Place

Living in your own home and community as you get older, safely and on your own terms, with the house and the help adjusted around you rather than you moving to fit a facility. The working definition we use comes from the [[aging-in-place-wikipedia|Wikipedia article]]: own home, safely, independently, comfortably, whatever the age or ability.

## What it means for us

It is not a slogan for "never move". It is a design problem with two parts that have to work together:

1. **The house.** Can a person with less strength, balance or eyesight get in, sleep, wash, cook and get out in an emergency without help? This is where [[grab-bar|grab bars]], lighting, rails, level entries and a bedroom and bathroom on the entry floor come in.
2. **The help.** Who does what the house cannot: shopping, laundry, appointments, a daily check that someone is fine. Family, neighbors and paid help, in some mix ([[informal-care-network]], [[home-care]]).

When either part fails, aging in place turns into isolation in a house that no longer fits. The honest version of the concept includes a point where staying is no longer the right answer; the [[care-ladder]] note lists the steps between.

## How it shapes Mom's house

Mom's house fails the first part in one important way: her only bedroom and full bathroom are upstairs ([[moms-house-layout-notes]]). [[denise-farrow]] said so on 2025-03-19 and nothing since has changed it. We have done what is cheap and reversible ([[moms-home-safety-upgrades]]: grab bars, a second stair rail, stair lighting, a pendant). The next physical step would be a [[stair-lift]] at $4,800 ([[is-a-stair-lift-worth-it-for-moms-house]]).

The second part is the [[home-care-aide-trial]]: two mornings a week of paid help on top of family and neighbors. Whether the two parts together are enough is exactly [[should-mom-stay-in-her-house]].

## How it shapes our own house

The same idea drove the hall bathroom remodel. At the [[bathroom-design-meeting-2025-11-18|bathroom design meeting]] we chose a curbless shower with a linear drain, a 34-inch door and blocking in every wall for future grab bars ([[wall-blocking]]) instead of a new tub, for $4,200 extra. Two reasons: Mom stays over, especially since her January 2025 fall, and we would like to stay in this house ourselves as we get older. Renata's resale worry was settled by keeping the tub in the primary bathroom. See [[hall-bathroom-remodel]] and the related stance [[build-for-the-next-twenty-years]].

Building it in during a remodel cost a few thousand dollars. Retrofitting the same thing at Mom's house would mean moving plumbing upstairs or adding a bathroom downstairs, which is why her options are now about the stairs and about moving.

## Limits we have noticed

- Aging in place depends on someone nearby. We are ten minutes away; Claire is four hours away. That asymmetry shapes [[claire-and-nathan-care-roles]].
- It can hide loneliness. The day program was meant to help with that; Mom did not want it ([[adult-day-program-trial]]).
- Costs creep. Two aide mornings are about $1,109 a month; more hours approach the cost of a move ([[housing-options-comparison]]).
- It is the person's choice, not the family's project ([[dignity-of-risk]], [[mom-decides-where-she-lives]]).
