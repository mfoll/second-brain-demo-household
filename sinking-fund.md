# Sinking Fund

A sinking fund, in our budget, is a monthly amount set aside for a cost we know is coming but that does not arrive monthly: car repairs, travel, gifts, insurance renewals, home maintenance, medical copays.

## How we use it

- One line per purpose in the [[household-budget-spreadsheet]], all held in one savings sub-account and tracked by line.
- Size each line by the expected annual cost divided by twelve, or by a target and a date: $3,000 of travel by July is $250 a month from October.
- Spending from a sinking fund is not a budget failure; that is what it is for. Overspending a sinking fund line is.
- Lines can be cut temporarily to fund something else, as the travel line was cut from $450 to $250 for the emergency fund rebuild. Write down the date it goes back.

## Why it matters to us

Before the [[household-budget-reset]], every renewal and repair felt like an emergency because nothing was set aside for it. Most of what we used to call emergencies were predictable costs with irregular timing.

## Not to confuse with

The emergency fund, which is for costs we cannot predict at all. A water heater failure is an emergency; tires every four years are a sinking fund.
