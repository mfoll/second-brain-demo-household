# Pick The Contractor Not The Lowest Bid

## Stance

When hiring for a house job, compare bids only after normalizing them to the same scope, then choose on references, named trades and how they handle changes. The lowest number on the page is often not the lowest price.

## Scope

Any job over about $5,000 with hidden work: renovation, roof, heating. For a like-for-like swap (a water heater, a fence) price matters more and a single trusted trade is fine.

## Why I Think This

Birchline bid $64,500 against Quillan's $71,400; with allowances priced to our choices it was about $72,800 and would have started three months later ([[renovation-bid-comparison-2026-01-12]]). Quillan then handled two change orders openly and in writing, and named a tile setter who did the one thing that could have gone badly wrong, the curbless shower. The neighbors' replies said the same thing about Birchline's allowances ([[neighborhood-contractor-recommendations]]).

## What Would Change My Mind

A job with a tight, fully specified scope where every bidder prices the same items and the trades are the same. Then the lowest bid is a fair choice.

## Implications

For the [[roof-replacement]]: ask every roofer to price the same shingle and the same decking allowance per sheet, and ask who will be on the roof.
