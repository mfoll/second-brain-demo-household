# Linear Drain

A long, narrow shower drain, usually along one wall, instead of a round drain in the middle of the floor. With a linear drain the floor only has to slope in one direction, which is what makes a curbless shower practical: the bathroom floor can run level into the shower and fall gently toward the back wall.

In our [[hall-bathroom-remodel]]:

- 36 inches long, at the back wall, as [[dana-whitlock]] suggested, so water runs away from the door.
- Floor recessed 3/4 inch between joists so the slope did not need a step at the entry.
- Liquid waterproofing membrane over the entire bathroom floor, not just the shower, because without a curb the whole floor is a potential wet area. Flood-tested 24 hours ([[sione-tupou]]).

Trade-offs: costs more than a center drain, needs a careful installer, and the grate needs a wipe every week or two. Large-format floor tile works with it, which also means fewer grout lines.
