# Cash-Timing Risk

The risk that a choice which is cheaper over a year demands money at a moment you cannot easily pay it. Total cost and cash timing are separate questions, and we used to answer only the first.

## Where it bit us

- Health plan: the HDHP is cheaper at every level of care ([[health-plan-break-even]]), but in 2026 it asked for $4,000 within about eight weeks, in the same season as the renovation change orders.
- Renovation: change orders are cheap relative to the contract and still arrive with two weeks' notice ([[how-should-we-fund-the-renovation-overrun]]).

## How we account for it now

- Every comparison gets a "peak quarter cash" row next to the annual total ([[health-plan-comparison-2027]]).
- A choice that shifts risk onto us is only acceptable when the emergency fund is at target ([[hdhp-needs-a-full-emergency-fund]]).
- Two big decisions in the same quarter get looked at together, not one at a time. The minivan in December and the HDHP in November would have been an example.

## Related

Sinking-fund lines in the budget are the other half of the answer: they turn predictable lumpy costs into monthly ones so only truly unpredictable costs hit the fund.
