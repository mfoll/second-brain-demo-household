# Open Enrollment Review 2026-10-21

Renata and I reviewed the 2027 Brackwater options and partly reversed last year's choice: back to the copay plan for 2027, but the HSA stays open and invested, and dental and vision stay as they are. Elections are due 2026-11-14.

## At a Glance

> [!success]
> Decision: copay plan for 2027, premium $330 a month higher than the high-deductible plan. HSA kept open and invested (balance $2,180). Dental and vision unchanged. Renata submits before 2026-11-14.

## What the 2026 plan year actually did

- Premiums: $3,720 for the year on the HDHP, against $7,680 on the copay plan.
- Employer seed: $1,000 into the HSA in January.
- Care: Eli's sports injury in 2026-04, then 14 physical therapy visits. The family deductible of $4,000 was used up by 2026-06 ([[deductible-met-2026-06-12]]). Coinsurance after that came to about $380 by October.
- Paid from the HSA: $1,600. Paid from savings: $2,400, which counted against the emergency fund along with the renovation overrun ([[emergency-fund-low-point-2026-08-31]]).

On a straight cost basis the HDHP still beat the copay plan for 2026 by about $1,500: $3,720 + $4,000 + $380 − $1,000 seed = $7,100, against roughly $7,680 + $560 in therapy copays + about $400 of other copays and deductible = $8,640. The plan did not fail on cost. Our 2025 high scenario had assumed we would reach the out-of-pocket maximum; we did not. It failed on timing: it needed $2,400 of cash in the same two months the renovation needed it.

## Discussion

- I came in wanting to stay on the HDHP one more year. My argument: 2026 was one injury, the expected-cost case still holds, and switching back loses the seed.
- Renata's argument: the condition we set in 2025 was a full emergency fund, and we do not have one until mid-2027. Eli is still on a sports team; the minivan decision may take cash in December; and paying $3,960 for a predictable year is a price she is willing to pay.
- What moved me: the cash column. The HDHP is cheaper in every scenario on paper, by $1,500 to $4,500, but a 2026-like year can demand $4,000 in one quarter, and the cost of being wrong while the fund is thin is a second year of borrowing from it. Her condition from 2025 was right and I had let it slide. We are paying, in effect, $1,500 to $4,500 for a predictable year.
- Dental and vision: no reason to change; [[hana-kovar]] is in network, and the vision plan covered Maya's and Eli's glasses without surprises.

## HSA

- The account stays open. We cannot contribute while on the copay plan, but the balance is ours and can pay eligible costs later.
- Balance $2,180 ([[hsa-account-summary-2026-10]]); Renata moved it into the administrator's broad index fund option on 2026-10-22, keeping $500 in cash. Open details in [[how-should-we-invest-the-hsa]].
- Receipts for the $2,400 we paid out of pocket in 2026 stay in Paperless-ngx; they can be reimbursed from the HSA in a later year if we need the cash.
