# Health Plan Break-Even

The level of annual care at which two health plans cost the same in total, premiums included. Below it the cheaper-premium plan wins; above it the richer plan wins.

## How to find it

Total cost = premiums + out-of-pocket spending at a given level of care − any employer contribution. Plot both plans against care from zero to beyond both out-of-pocket maximums and look for the crossing.

## What we learned

For the two Brackwater plans there is no crossing. The HDHP's premium saving plus the $1,000 seed ($4,960 a year) is larger than the gap between the two out-of-pocket maximums ($2,000). The HDHP is cheaper at every level of care.

That does not make it the right plan every year. A plan that is always cheaper can still be the wrong one if it demands cash you do not have at the time you need it ([[cash-timing-risk]]). That is the lesson of 2026 ([[open-enrollment-review-2026-10-21]]).

## Check each year

If the premium gap narrows or the HDHP's maximum rises, a crossing can appear. Recompute when the benefits guide comes out.
