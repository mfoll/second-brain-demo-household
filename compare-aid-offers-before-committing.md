# Compare Aid Offers Before Committing

## Stance

We do not let a child commit to a college before we can lay out at least three aid offers side by side and compare [[net-price]]. No binding early decision, no deposit, no loan acceptance before then.

## Scope

College choice for Maya in 2026-27 and, by default, Eli in 2028-29. It does not stop early action or applying early; it stops binding commitments.

## Why I Think This

- Calculators gave estimates for Maya's list from about $16,900 to $31,000 a year; actual offers can differ by thousands either way ([[college-cost-comparison]]).
- An early decision offer is judged "acceptable" against nothing.
- Our cash position in 2026-27 is not strong: emergency fund rebuilding ([[emergency-fund-rebuild]]), a car decision, the health premium rise ([[open-enrollment-review-2026-10-21]]).
- At the [[financial-aid-night-2026-10-08]] the school's own advice was to compare offers, and to treat loans as cost.

## What Would Change My Mind

- A savings position that covers four years at the most expensive school on the list.
- A school offering a guaranteed full-need policy with a published formula we can run ourselves.

## Implications

- [[should-maya-apply-early-decision]] answered no.
- The decision on where Maya enrolls waits for offers in spring 2027 ([[how-will-we-pay-for-mayas-first-year]]).
