# Home Energy Retrofits

Exploration cluster: reading about making the house cheaper to heat and run, with no Project yet. It started when the heat pump water heater cut our gas bill and the 30% national home energy credit paid back $735 of it ([[water-heater-replacement-2025-05-21]]).

## What is here

- [[heat-pump]] (the water heater article redirects there) and [[building-insulation]]: background reading.
- [[heat-pump-payback]]: my rough model for whether a replacement pays.
- [[water-heater-energy-use-comparison]]: our own before and after numbers.
- [[should-we-replace-the-furnace-with-a-heat-pump]]: the open question.

## Current thinking

The furnace is a 2007 gas unit, 19 years old, still working, serviced every fall. Replacing it on failure would mean a rushed choice in January, which is how the water heater went. The 200-amp panel from the kitchen work ([[panel-upgrade-change-order-2026-08-12]]) removed one obstacle. The credit is capped at $2,000 a year, so a heat pump and attic insulation in different tax years would claim more than both in one.

## When this becomes a Project

When we have money for it, which is after the [[emergency-fund-rebuild]] reaches $28,000 (target 2027-06-30). Until then it stays reading.
