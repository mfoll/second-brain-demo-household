# HDHP Needs A Full Emergency Fund

## Stance

A high-deductible plan is the right choice for us only in a year when the emergency fund is at target and no other large cash demand is expected. Otherwise we pay the higher premium for a predictable year, even though the HDHP is cheaper on paper.

## Scope

Our family, on Brackwater's two plans as designed in 2026 and 2027. Different plan designs, a much larger premium gap, or a different family situation could change it.

## Why I Think This

- 2026: the HDHP saved about $1,500 over the year and still put $2,400 of unplanned cost on savings in two months ([[deductible-met-2026-06-12]]).
- The cost advantage is real but bounded ($1,500 to $4,500 a year); the cash demand can be $4,000 in a quarter ([[health-plan-comparison-2027]]).
- We had written the condition down in 2025 and then ignored it when the renovation started. Writing it as a standing rule makes it harder to ignore.

This replaces my 2025 stance, [[hdhp-is-the-default-for-a-healthy-family]], which treated the decision as expected cost only.

## What Would Change My Mind

- The emergency fund at $28,000 or more for six months.
- An employer seed large enough to cover most of the deductible.
- A premium gap above about $500 a month, where the saving starts to outweigh the timing risk even with a thin fund.

## Implications

- Revisit at the 2028 enrollment, not before.
- Keep the HSA invested; it is the bridge if we return ([[how-should-we-invest-the-hsa]]).
