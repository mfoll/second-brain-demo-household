## Working Context

The 2026 high-deductible plan was a reasonable bet that went against us in cash-flow terms. On paper it still came out about $1,500 cheaper than the copay plan for 2026 (premium savings plus the $1,000 employer seed, against a fully used $4,000 deductible), but it put $2,400 of unplanned spending on us in May and June, from savings, while the renovation was already over budget. With the emergency fund at $17,360 and a car decision due 2026-12-15, we chose a predictable year. The full comparison, three spending scenarios, is in [[health-plan-comparison-2027]]. My earlier stance is archived as [[hdhp-is-the-default-for-a-healthy-family]]; the current one is [[hdhp-needs-a-full-emergency-fund]].

Renata leaned to the copay plan from the start of October. I wanted to stay on the HDHP for one more year and argued the 2026 spending was a one-off. What changed my mind was the cash column, not the cost column: the HDHP is cheaper in every scenario, but a 2026-like year can demand up to $4,000 in one quarter, and that would come out of a fund still being rebuilt.
