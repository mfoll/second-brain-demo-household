# Insure Catastrophes, Not Repairs

## Stance

Buy insurance for losses we could not absorb (liability, the house burning down, a serious accident) and self-insure the ones we could (repairs, small claims, an old car's damage). Use high deductibles where the premium saving is real, and keep the cash to cover them.

## Scope

Home, auto, umbrella and appliance coverage. Health is the exception where the cash-timing problem is sharp enough to change the answer in some years ([[hdhp-needs-a-full-emergency-fund]]).

## Why I Think This

- Small-loss coverage is priced to cost more than it pays on average; the insurer's margin is the price of not having to think.
- We have sinking funds and an emergency fund precisely so that a $2,500 home deductible or a dead appliance is a budget event, not a crisis.
- Examples: home deductible raised to $2,500 in 2025 ([[home-insurance-policy]]); collision dropped on the Tessera ([[should-we-drop-collision-on-the-tessera]]); no extended warranties on the Kestova appliances ([[skip-extended-warranties]]); umbrella kept and probably raised ([[should-we-raise-the-umbrella-limit]]).

## What Would Change My Mind

- An emergency fund that stays below three months for long.
- A claim history where small losses are frequent rather than rare.

## Implications

Every new policy or warranty offer gets the same question: could we pay this loss from the fund without borrowing? If yes, skip or raise the deductible.
