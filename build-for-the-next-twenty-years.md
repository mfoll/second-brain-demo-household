# Build For The Next Twenty Years

## Stance

When we change the house, we build for how we will live in it at 65, not only how we live now. If a feature makes the house work for an older or injured person and costs little extra while the walls are open, we do it.

## Scope

Our own house: bathrooms, stairs, doorways, lighting, kitchen heights. Not a reason to gold-plate; resale still counts.

## Why I Think This

- Mom's January 2025 fall and the work at her house afterwards ([[moms-home-safety-upgrades]]) showed how expensive and disruptive retrofits are once they are urgent. Her only bathroom is upstairs, which is now part of the question of whether she can stay ([[should-mom-stay-in-her-house]]).
- The curbless shower, wide door and blocking added $4,200 to a $71,400 job ([[bathroom-design-meeting-2025-11-18]]). Retrofitting the same later would cost several times that.
- Good design for older people is mostly good design for everyone ([[universal-design]]).
- We plan to stay here at least twenty years ([[aging-in-place]]).

## What Would Change My Mind

A decision to move within about ten years, in which case resale preferences dominate. Renata holds this a little more loosely than I do; the primary bathroom tub stays ([[should-the-primary-bathroom-keep-its-tub]]).

## Implications

Next jobs: brighter stair lighting and a second stair rail at our house when the roof is done; lever handles as doors are replaced.
