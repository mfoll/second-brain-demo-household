# Springing Versus Immediate Authority

A power of attorney can take effect immediately on signing, or "spring" into effect only when a condition is met, usually a doctor certifying that the person cannot manage their affairs.

- **Immediate:** the agent can act the day it is signed, even while the principal is fully capable. Simple to use; requires trust.
- **Springing:** protects against the agent acting too early, but in a crisis someone has to obtain the certification first, which takes time exactly when time is short.

[[harold-iwasaki]] explained both. Mom chose an immediate financial power of attorney so Nathan could pay a bill or talk to her bank if she was in hospital, without paperwork. The [[healthcare-proxy]] works the other way by nature: it only applies if she cannot decide herself.

Nathan has used the financial power of attorney twice, both times at Mom's request: once to speak to her home insurer, once to move savings between her own accounts.
