# How Should We Fund The Renovation Overrun?

Raised 2026-07-08 with the first change order; answered 2026-08-12 with the second.

## Current Answer

> [!success]
> From the emergency fund, not a second HELOC draw. The $6,240 overrun came out of the fund, which (with the $2,400 health deductible gap) fell from $26,000 to $17,360. The [[emergency-fund-rebuild]] restores it to $28,000 by 2027-06-30.

## Options Considered

| Option | Cost | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Second HELOC draw | Variable interest, about $40 a month on $6,240 | Adds debt on the house for a gap we could cover in cash; the rate can rise |
| Emergency fund | Lost interest only | Fund drops below three months of expenses for about a year |
| Cut scope (drop under-cabinet lights, cheaper backsplash) | Saves about $1,400 | Small saving, worse kitchen, and Quillan would credit only part |
| Pause the travel sinking fund | $450 a month | Halvane was already booked and paid |

Renata argued for the emergency fund on the basis that it exists for exactly this kind of surprise and we have stable jobs. I wanted to keep the fund intact and draw on the HELOC. Her case won once we priced the rebuild: about $890 a month for twelve months.
