# Teens Pay A Share Of Their Costs

## Stance

Once a teenager has income, they pay a visible share of the costs their independence creates, not the whole cost. For Maya that is $30 a month of the $1,140 a year insurance increase (about a third) and her own fuel when the tank drops below a quarter.

## Scope

Costs that come from a privilege: driving, phone upgrades beyond the family plan, optional trips. Not school fees, activity fees, college applications or anything health-related; those are ours.

## Why I Think This

- It makes the cost real without making it a barrier. Maya earns about $1,100 a month in summer and $600 in the school year from her weekend job.
- Renata and I both paid for our own first cars' insurance in full at 18; we think that was too much at 17.
- It keeps the conversation about driving rules practical: she knows what a ticket would cost the family ([[house-driving-agreement]]).

## Disagreement

Renata thinks a third is too low and would go to half once Maya is 18. I think the share should stay while she is in school. Revisit at the review on 2027-03-18.

## What Would Change My Mind

If the job starts competing with school or swim, we drop the contribution rather than have her take more shifts.

## Implications

- Eli will get the same rule when he drives.
- The $30 is a transfer into the household account on the 1st, visible in the [[household-budget-spreadsheet]].
