# Renovation Overrun Household Impact

What the renovation going $6,240 over plan cost us outside the renovation itself. The renovation numbers are in [[renovation-budget-tracker]]; this note is about the knock-on effects, written 2026-09-07 and kept current.

## Direct

- Emergency fund paid the $6,240 overrun, which together with the $2,400 health deductible gap took it from $26,000 to $17,360 ([[emergency-fund-low-point-2026-08-31]]).
- Rebuild: $890 a month until 2027-06-30 to reach $28,000 ([[emergency-fund-rebuild]]).

## Knock-on

- Travel sinking fund cut from $450 to $250 a month; spring break 2027 now has a smaller budget.
- Will update paused, mainly to defer Harold's fee; appointment moved to 2027-01-19 ([[will-update-paused-2026-08-16]]).
- Health plan: the thin fund was the main reason we left the HDHP for 2027, at about $3,960 a year in higher premiums ([[open-enrollment-review-2026-10-21]]).
- Minivan: Renata's case for the $3,400 repair over a $27,000 SUV is mostly about protecting the rebuild ([[should-maya-drive-the-old-minivan]]).

## Rough cost of the overrun beyond $6,240

Higher health premiums for 2027 ($3,960, partly offset by lower risk) and three or four months' delay in reaching the fund target. None of these is large on its own. Together they are why I now want a 10% contingency in any project plan, not the 0% we effectively had above the contract.
